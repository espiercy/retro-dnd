# Rules Cyclopedia V1 Scope Audit

## Purpose

This is a coverage-traceability companion to `docs/rules/INVENTORY.md`, demonstrating that the rebuilt inventory was derived from broad, deliberate coverage of the *Dungeons & Dragons Rules Cyclopedia* (TSR 1071, 1991) rather than from the previous 1974-primary inventory relabeled. It is not rules authority and does not itself establish any mechanic — see `SOURCE_HIERARCHY.md` for that.

## Verification Method and Its Limits

Unlike the 1974 three-book core, which this project extracted page-by-page from a digitized reproduction (`docs/rules/exploration/dungeon_wandering_monster_check.md` §"1974 Source"), no equivalent clean, complete primary-text extraction of the Rules Cyclopedia was available to this task through the tools in hand: a full-text archive.org stream and one PDF host returned connection/access errors, and a second PDF exceeded this task's fetch size limit. Coverage below is therefore built from this project's own trained knowledge of the Rules Cyclopedia's well-documented structure, cross-checked against multiple independent secondary sources (Wikipedia, the unofficial *Rules Cyclopedia Companion* errata/reference site, and several specialist OSR forum threads — Dragonsfoot, EN World) for the specific facts this inventory relies on.

**This is chapter/section-level confidence, not page-verified primary-text confidence**, consistent with `SOURCE_HIERARCHY.md` §17's "where reliably available" allowance and this task's own explicitly shallow, breadth-first mandate (`docs/rules/INVENTORY.md`'s assigning instructions §5). Every entry below and in `INVENTORY.md` that rests on this method, rather than a directly quoted primary passage, should be treated as **provisional structural knowledge requiring primary-text verification during that entry's own future Rule Card research** — not as settled fact. Where a specific number or rule could not be corroborated with reasonable confidence, it is flagged explicitly rather than asserted.

## Book Structure (as documented across sources)

The Rules Cyclopedia is a 304-page hardcover compiling and revising the D&D Basic, Expert, Companion, and Master boxed sets (not the separate Immortals Rules, which remains a distinct, uncompiled product and is therefore outside this book's own scope entirely — no audit or inventory action needed for it). It is organized into roughly four major parts: a Player's-Manual-equivalent (character creation, spells, equipment, combat basics), a Dungeon-Master's-Rulebook-equivalent (running the game, dungeon/wilderness adventures, monsters, treasure, NPCs), Wilderness Adventures material (overland travel, exploration, strongholds), and Dominion Rules (domain management, mass combat/"War Machine"). It supports character levels 1–36.

## Honest Completeness Assessment

This audit does not claim exhaustive, page-perfect coverage of a 304-page compiled rulebook using web-search-corroborated structural knowledge in place of a primary-text extraction. Confidence is high for the major system list (classes, combat shape, saving-throw categories, spell-level range, level cap, optional-system identification, training's absence) because each of those was independently corroborated by at least one, and usually several, specific secondary sources beyond general background knowledge. Confidence is lower, and explicitly flagged as such in `INVENTORY.md`, for exact cadence/frequency numbers this pass could not pin down (the dungeon wandering-monster check interval chief among them) and for whether RC's dungeon-generation guidance is procedural enough to retire `SIM-001`.

**Areas most likely to need a second coverage pass once primary-text access is available:** the exact table of contents and page numbers (this audit cites at chapter/section granularity throughout, not page numbers, for exactly this reason); the full extent of high-level Fighter sub-classes beyond Paladin/Avenger, if any; the precise mechanical shape of Weapon Mastery's five tiers and General Skills' resolution beyond the structural summary already captured; and whether any Wilderness Adventures content beyond the narrow movement/pursuit facts already flagged turns out to be an unavoidable V1 dependency.
