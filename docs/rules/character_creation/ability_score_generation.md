## Rules Cyclopedia Explicitly Establishes

1. **Die method, and a standing range limitation.** Each ability score is generated by rolling three six-sided dice and summing them; the resulting range is 3–18. RC states the bound in its own words at p. 6: *"if you roll ones each time, then the total score is 3, the least score possible. If you roll all sixes, then the total is 18, **the highest score you can have**… You should end up with six numbers, each between 3 and 18."* RC p. 130 establishes that the bound is a property of **ability scores** rather than of the dice, by invoking it for a procedure in which the player *assigns* values instead of rolling them: *"Of course, **the range limitation of 3 to 18 for ability scores** still applies."* This is the derivation basis for §4 **R11**.
2. **The six abilities and their order**, as RC lists them: Strength, Intelligence, Wisdom, Dexterity, Constitution, Charisma.
3. **Roll-in-place.** The baseline procedure records each roll next to the ability it was rolled for. RC offers no arrange-to-taste option in the Chapter 1 procedure.
4. **Sequencing.** Score generation (step 1) is separated from score adjustment (step 3) by class choice (step 2). RC states the class must be decided first, because only the prime requisite may be raised, and the prime requisite is a property of the class.
5. **The 2-for-1 trade** (p. 7), verbatim in the summary box: *"You can trade two points from an ability score you don't want to raise your prime requisite by one point. You can perform this trade as many times as you want, so long as you do not lower any ability below a score of 9. You cannot lower your Dexterity, Constitution, or Charisma. You can only raise your character's prime requisite score."*
6. **The four numbered trade rules** (p. 7): (1) the prime requisite goes up 1 for each 2 another score goes down, and if a class has two prime requisites both can be raised; (2) Constitution and Charisma points cannot be exchanged with others; (3) Dexterity cannot be lowered, but it may be raised for a thief or halfling character; (4) no score can be lowered below 9, and if it is already 10 or less it cannot be lowered.
7. **The trade is optional and time-boxed** (p. 7): *"If you want to exchange any ability score points, you must do that now before you go any further in making the character. No such adjustments can be made later. Remember that you do not have to adjust any ability scores. This is an option, not a requirement."*
8. **Above-1st-level generation is a different procedure** (p. 130, Step 2). RC states the ordinary 3d6 method *may* be used but "this arbitrary method may result in conflicts with the class chosen," and offers two alternatives: **First Method (Rolling and Assigning)** — roll 3d6 eight times, keep the six best, assign in any order; **Second Method (Point Allocation)** — the DM gives 60 + 5d6 points, or an equal allotment of at least 60 and no more than 90, which the player distributes, with the 3–18 range still applying.
9. **Two DM-discretionary provisions exist outside Chapter 1** (p. 145): a **discard** provision and a **score-switch** provision. The switch is stated narrowly: *"Just switch the highest score rolled for the character to the Prime Requisite ability appropriate to the class the player wants."*

## Rules Cyclopedia Leaves Undefined / Ambiguous

Four items, each narrow. **All four are now settled for this project by human ruling (SR-2, SR-3, SR-4 — see Simulator Ruling below), but the underlying RC ambiguity is real and is recorded here rather than papered over.** What follows is what RC does and does not say; what the simulator does about it is in the specification.

**U1 — The discard criterion has two non-equivalent formulations in one passage (p. 145).**
- *"When a newly created character has all low scores (all scores under 9), the player should be allowed to discard it."* → every score ≤ 8.
- *"…a poor character, with no score above 9 or two scores below 6; such characters should be discarded."* → (max score ≤ 9) **or** (at least two scores ≤ 5).

These differ at a score of exactly 9, and the second adds an independent trigger the first lacks. Both are permissive, and both are overridden by player choice (*"a player might want to play this character; if he does, let him"*).

**U2 — The relationship between the Chapter 1 trade and the Chapter 13 switch is unstated.** RC gives no ordering, no cross-reference, and does not say whether Chapter 1's "no such adjustments can be made later" binds a provision printed in a DM-facing chapter 138 pages away. At least two executable orderings exist (switch before class choice; switch after class choice but before the trade), and they produce different characters.

**U3 — Whether a Mystic may raise Dexterity through the trade.** Rule 3's exception names *"a thief or halfling character."* The p. 7 table on the same page gives the Mystic prime requisites **Strength and Dexterity**, and the summary box's general principle is *"You can only raise your character's prime requisite score."* The general principle admits the Mystic; the enumeration omits it. Mystic is required V1 content (`DEC-0008`), so this is live.

**U4 — Whether the switch may be granted to satisfy a class ability minimum.** The *mechanical envelope* is settled (see Approved Mechanical Specification §6.3) but RC's stated trigger is a class-preference mismatch, and RC never says whether meeting a demihuman/Mystic minimum is a permitted use.

## Simulator Ruling

**Four required, escalated, and GRANTED by the human project owner on 2026-08-29.** Each is stated separately and **not bundled** (`RULE_CARD_RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md` §16).

> An earlier draft paired *"Simulator Rulings: none required"* with *"U3 blocks Mystic trades"* while calling everything non-blocking. That was incoherent and is corrected: U3 did require a ruling, and it has one.

### SR-2 — a Mystic **may** raise Dexterity through the 2-for-1 trade (closes U3)

**Missing behavior.** Whether the Mystic may raise Dexterity, which is one of its two prime requisites.

**Why an answer is required.** The Mystic is required V1 content (`DEC-0008`); the trade is standard creation behaviour. Without a ruling the step cannot execute for a legal V1 character.

**Why RC does not answer it.** RC p. 7's box states the general permission — *"You can only raise your character's prime requisite score"* — and its numbered rule 3 states *"Dexterity cannot be lowered (but it may be raised for a thief or halfling character)."* The p. 7 table on the same page gives the Mystic prime requisites **Strength and Dexterity**. The general principle admits the Mystic; the enumeration omits it.

**Why compatible sources do not answer it.** The Mystic is **not a BECMI core class** — the Master Set offers it only as *"suggestions… for converting a new monster, the mystic, into a character class if the DM desires"* (Master Players' Book p. 2, visually verified). No BECMI volume carries a Mystic prime-requisite trade rule. A positive determination from an inspected object, not an assumption.

**The smallest ruling.** RC's general two-prime-requisite rule — *"If a character has two prime requisites, both can be raised"* — applies to the Mystic's Strength and Dexterity. The thief/halfling wording in rule 3 is treated as an **editorial carryover not updated when the Mystic was integrated**. **No Mystic-only exchange rate, exception, or new adjustment rule is created**; every ordinary constraint (R1–R9) applies unchanged, including that Dexterity may never be *lowered*.

### SR-3 — canonical ordering of the Chapter 13 switch (closes U2, U4, and `CHAR-002` V1)

**Missing behavior.** RC states the Chapter 1 trade and the Chapter 13 switch in different chapters with no cross-reference and no stated order.

**Scope decision, first.** The Chapter 13 switch is **INCLUDED IN V1**. It is an explicit RC procedure and is not dropped merely for being DM-discretionary. Its **discretionary character is preserved** — see §6.2.

**The ruling.** The switch is evaluated **before** eligibility and class choice; the 2-for-1 trade **after**. Full sequence at §0.

**Consequence, and the distinction that matters.** The switch is **eligibility-shaping**: it can establish a raw class minimum where the switched score is the required ability. The trade is **post-eligibility**: it cannot retroactively make a character eligible for a class already chosen. §0 and §6.3 state this.

### SR-5 — class eligibility invariant (closes the Mystic Wisdom edge case)

**Missing behavior.** Whether the Chapter 1 trade may reduce an ability below a creation minimum required by the class the character has already selected.

**Why an answer is required.** Without one, a legal V1 character can be produced that does not satisfy its own class's stated requirements: qualify as a Mystic on Wisdom 13, select Mystic, then trade Wisdom down to 12 and finish creation as a Mystic with Wisdom 12.

**Why RC does not answer it.** RC's p. 7 exchange restrictions protect Constitution, Charisma and Dexterity. **They do not protect Wisdom**, and RC never states that a class's creation minimums must survive the trade — the trade and the requirements table are stated independently and never composed. RC's own p. 7 worked example lowers an **elf's** Wisdom to 9, but that does **not** resolve this case: Wisdom is not an Elf creation requirement, so the example demonstrates nothing about a gated ability.

**The smallest ruling.**

```text
SR-5 — CLASS ELIGIBILITY INVARIANT

The Chapter 1 2-for-1 prime-requisite adjustment may not reduce
any ability score below a minimum required for the character's
already-selected class.
```

Recorded as trade rule **R10** in §4. It adds one constraint to an existing procedure; **no new adjustment rule, exchange rate, or class-specific exception is created**, and no other card changes.

**What it does not do.** It does **not** reopen eligibility and does **not** let the trade establish eligibility. `CHAR-002`'s result is an **invariant the trade must preserve**, not a test the trade may re-run.

### SR-4 — discard criterion (closes U1)

**Missing behavior.** RC p. 145 states the discard condition twice, non-equivalently.

**The ruling.** The **detailed** formulation governs: the player may be offered a discard when **no ability score is above 9**, **or** at least **two** ability scores are **below 6**. The earlier "all scores under 9" wording is treated as an **introductory summary**, not an exclusive competing criterion. RC's own override is preserved: **the player may keep the character anyway.**

## Approved Mechanical Specification

> Terminology: **as-rolled scores** are the six values produced by §1. **Eligibility scores** are the values after any authorized §6.2 switch — these are what `CHAR-002` evaluates. **Adjusted scores** are the values after the §4 trade. Where no switch is authorized, eligibility scores are identical to as-rolled scores.

### 0. Canonical V1 character-creation ordering (SR-3)

**This is rules ordering only. It specifies no orchestration object, and none may be inferred from it.**

```text
1. Roll six ability scores in place                        §1
        ↓
2. Discard / replacement determination                     §6.1  (SR-4)
        ↓   (if discarded, return to step 1)
3. DM / simulation policy MAY authorize the Ch.13
   highest-score switch for the desired class              §6.2  (SR-3)
        ↓
4. Evaluate creation eligibility on the scores
   AFTER any authorized switch                             CHAR-002
        ↓
5. Choose a class from the eligible set                    CHAR-002
        ↓
6. Optional 2-for-1 prime-requisite trade                  §4
   — must preserve the class's creation minimums    R10  (SR-5)
        ↓
7. Re-derive prime-requisite / XP effects                  ADV-001
```

**The load-bearing distinction:**

| | Chapter 13 switch (step 3) | Chapter 1 trade (step 6) |
|---|---|---|
| Role | **Eligibility-shaping** | **Post-eligibility** |
| Runs | **before** eligibility is evaluated | **after** a class is chosen |
| Can it **establish** a class minimum? | **YES** — where the switched score is the required ability | **NO** — it can never make a character eligible for a class already chosen |
| Can it **destroy** an established minimum? | n/a — it runs before eligibility | **NO** — forbidden by R10 (SR-5) |
| Gated by | DM / simulation policy | Nothing; it is the player's option |

```text
Eligibility is established BEFORE the trade — and must remain true AFTER it.
```

Step 7 exists because the trade can change a prime requisite, which changes the `ADV-001` XP modifier. **`CHAR-001` does not compute that modifier** — it only notes that the value it feeds may have changed.

### 1. Standard generation (1st-level characters) — the baseline procedure

```text
FOR each ability IN [Strength, Intelligence, Wisdom, Dexterity, Constitution, Charisma]:
    score(ability) = sum of three independent d6 rolls
```

- Exactly six abilities, generated in the order listed.
- Each score is recorded against the ability it was rolled for. **No reordering, reassignment, or rerolling occurs at this step.**
- Domain of each score: integer 3–18 inclusive. **This is RC's standing range limitation for ability scores, not merely the arithmetic range of 3d6** — RC applies the same limitation to player-assigned values (p. 130). §4 **R11** applies it to the trade.
- Total: six 3d6 rolls, eighteen d6 results, drawn from the simulation RNG stream (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §5; `docs/technical/RNG_CONTRACT.md`).

**RC provides no reroll of an individual score.** The only recreate provision RC offers is the whole-character discard of §6.1.

### 2. Class choice

Class is chosen after §1 and before §4. Class choice, its ability minimums, and its prime requisites are **`CHAR-002`'s** responsibility; this card consumes the chosen class's prime requisite(s) as an input to §4 and does not restate the eligibility rules.

Prime requisites, reproduced from the p. 7 table **for the trade's use only** (authoritative statement belongs to `CHAR-002`):

| Class | Prime requisite(s) |
|---|---|
| Cleric | Wisdom |
| Fighter | Strength |
| Magic-User | Intelligence |
| Thief | Dexterity |
| Dwarf | Strength |
| Elf | Strength, Intelligence |
| Halfling | Strength, Dexterity |
| Mystic | Strength, Dexterity |
| Druid | Wisdom *(not reachable at creation — `CHAR-002`)* |

### 3. Eligibility is evaluated on eligibility scores, never on adjusted scores

Eligibility (`CHAR-002`) consumes the scores **after** any authorized §6.2 switch and **before** the §4 trade (SR-3, §0 step 4).

**Necessary mechanical consequence, derived and shown:** the trade may raise only a prime requisite (R1); Constitution and Charisma may not be exchanged at all (R3); and class must be chosen before the trade (RC p. 7). **Therefore the trade can never create eligibility for a class already chosen** — the eligibility test has already run. The dwarf's Constitution 9 gate in particular can never be reached by trade, because Constitution cannot be exchanged at all.

The switch, by contrast, runs *before* the test and **can** establish a minimum — bounded by §6.3.

### 4. The prime-requisite trade (optional)

Applied after class choice. The character's player may perform any number of trades, each:

```text
choose donor  ability D
choose target ability T
score(D) -= 2
score(T) += 1
```

subject to **all** of:

| # | Rule | RC source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | `T` must be a prime requisite of the chosen class | p. 7 box; rule 1 |
| R2 | If the class has two prime requisites, **both** are eligible targets | p. 7 rule 1 |
| R3 | `D` may not be Constitution or Charisma | p. 7 rule 2 |
| R4 | `D` may not be Dexterity | p. 7 rule 3 and box |
| R5 | `D` may not be an ability that is not being lowered *for* a legal target — i.e. only `T` is raised; no other score increases | p. 7 box |
| R6 | After the trade, `score(D)` must be ≥ 9. Equivalently, a trade from `D` is legal only if `score(D)` ≥ 11 before it | p. 7 rule 4 |
| R7 | If `score(D)` is already 10 or less, `D` cannot be lowered at all | p. 7 rule 4 |
| R8 | The trade is entirely optional | p. 7 |
| R9 | The trade occurs at this step only; no such adjustment may be made later | p. 7 |
| **R10** | **After the trade, every creation-eligibility minimum of the selected class must still be satisfied** | **SR-5** — not RC-explicit |
| **R11** | **After the trade, `score(T)` must be ≤ 18.** A trade that would raise the target ability above 18 is **rejected** | **Necessary Mechanical Consequence** — RC p. 6 states the 3–18 bound for ability scores; RC p. 130 applies that same *"range limitation of 3 to 18 for ability scores"* to player-**assigned** values. Not RC-explicit at p. 7 |

**R10 — the class eligibility invariant (SR-5).** RC's restrictions at R3/R4 protect Constitution, Charisma and Dexterity, but **not** every ability that gates a class. Without R10 a character could qualify for a class and then trade away the score that qualified it. R10 closes that:

```text
Eligibility is established BEFORE the trade  (§0 step 4)
                and must remain true AFTER it  (R10)
```

**R10 does not reopen eligibility, and it does not let the trade create eligibility.** The asymmetry is deliberate:

| | Ch. 13 switch (§6.2) | Ch. 1 trade (§4) |
|---|---|---|
| Can **establish** eligibility | **Yes** | **No** |
| Can **destroy** established eligibility | n/a — runs before | **No** (R10) |

**Where R10 actually binds.** Only where a class's creation minimum sits on an ability the ordinary restrictions leave unprotected:

| Class | Creation minimum | Already protected? | R10 binds? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dwarf | Constitution 9 | Yes — R3 | No |
| Elf | Intelligence 9 | No, but Intelligence is an Elf **prime requisite**, so it can only be raised (R1) | No |
| Halfling | Dexterity 9 | Yes — R4 | No |
| Halfling | Constitution 9 | Yes — R3 | No |
| Mystic | Dexterity 13 | Yes — R4 | No |
| **Mystic** | **Wisdom 13** | **No** — Wisdom is not a Mystic prime requisite and is not covered by R3 or R4 | **YES** |

**The Mystic's Wisdom is the one materially binding case**, and R10 exists for it. SR-2 is unaffected: a Mystic may still **raise** Dexterity through the ordinary trade.

**R11 — the prime-requisite ceiling.** RC's p. 7 exchange rules bound the **donor** (R6/R7) but state no bound on the **target**. The bound comes from outside p. 7, by composition of two RC-explicit statements:

```text
RC p. 6    ability scores run 3-18; 18 is "the highest score you can have"
RC p. 130  that bound is "the range limitation of 3 to 18 for ability scores",
           and it "still applies" where the player ASSIGNS values rather than
           rolling them
§4         the trade assigns a new value to an ability score
    ∴      a trade result above 18 is illegal
```

**Rejected reading, preserved rather than erased.** *The p. 7 numbered trade rules are exhaustive, so their silence about an upper target bound permits scores above 18.* That reading is not baseless — RC states the **floor** twice at p. 7 (rule 4 gives both *"no score can be lowered below 9"* and the 10-or-less restatement), which shows RC was willing to be redundant about bounds it cared about, and RC never composes the range limitation with the trade at the trade's own location. **The project does not adopt it** because RC elsewhere establishes 3–18 as a standing range limitation *for ability scores* and applies it to player-assigned values, and a standing property of ability scores does not need restating in every section that produces one.

**This is composition of RC rules, not selection among contradictory rules.** No RC statement is set aside, no reading is chosen over a competing RC statement, and **R11 is therefore not a Simulator Ruling, not a Human-Approved Variant, and not an Alternate-Source Compatible Completion.** It is equally **not verbatim RC-Explicit for the trade** — RC nowhere writes *"a trade may not raise a score above 18."*

**R11 does not disturb RC's own worked example.** RC p. 7's second example raises a cleric's Wisdom from 15 to exactly 18. R11 binds **above** 18, not at it, so that example remains reproducible unchanged — asserted by **T13** and guarded by **C5**.

**R11 and R10 are independent, and neither modifies the other:**

| | Binds | Side of the trade |
|---|---|---|
| **R10** (SR-5) | the selected class's creation minimums must survive the trade | **floor** side |
| **R11** | the target may not exceed 18 | **ceiling** side |

**Residual risk, stated plainly.** The derivation reads p. 130's sentence as *invoking* a Chapter 1 limitation rather than *creating* a Chapter 10 one. The word *"still"* is what settles that, and it is the point a future reviewer should test first.

**R6 and R7 are consistent, not redundant** (arithmetic shown): a trade removes 2 points, so 11 → 9 is legal and 10 → 8 would breach the floor. R7 is RC restating R6's consequence at the boundary.

**Dexterity as a target.** Dexterity may be **raised** wherever it is a prime requisite of the chosen class — **Thief, Halfling, and Mystic**. RC's rule 3 enumerates only the thief and halfling; **SR-2** rules that the general two-prime-requisite principle governs and that the enumeration is an editorial carryover from before the Mystic was integrated. Dexterity may **never be lowered** for any class, Mystic included (R4), and **no Mystic-specific exchange rate or exception exists** — R1–R9 apply unchanged.

### 5. Above-1st-level generation (Chapter 10) — a separate, DM-facing procedure

**This is not an alternative to §1 for ordinary 1st-level characters and must never be presented as evidence that baseline generation is non-random.** It applies only when the DM is creating a character that starts above 1st level (RC p. 130, Step 2), and RC frames it as DM choice.

| Method | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Ordinary** | 3d6 per ability as §1. RC notes it "may result in conflicts with the class chosen." |
| **First Method — Rolling and Assigning** | Roll 3d6 **eight** times; keep the **six best** results; assign them to abilities **in any order**. |
| **Second Method — Point Allocation** | The DM supplies a point total: either **60 + 5d6**, or an equal allotment of **at least 60 and at most 90**. The player distributes the total across the six abilities. The 3–18 range per ability still applies. |

Method Two is **non-random**. Its existence is recorded because a Stage-A negative finding asserting RC contains no non-random alternative was falsified (`CLUSTER-002-completeness-audit.md` Finding 7).

**V1 default:** the standard §1 procedure. §5 is specified so it is not lost, and is **not** wired to any V1 flow by this card.

**Implementation scope (human direction, 2026-09-05).** §5 **is** within `CLUSTER-002` implementation scope. It is to be implemented as **pure rules functions** and to remain **unwired** from the ordinary 1st-level V1 creation flow. *"Not V1-wired" does not mean "not implemented"*: **H1–H5 behaviour is implemented, and H6's unreachability is preserved** as the guard that §1 remains the only 1st-level generation path. **No character-creation engine, builder, or orchestration object is authorized** by this direction.

Note that **H4** — rejecting an allocation of 19 *"the 3–18 range still applies"* — has been in this card since its first Stage-B draft. It is the same standing limitation that §4 **R11** now applies to the trade; before R11, this card answered the same question two different ways in two different sections.

### 6. Chapter 13 provisions (p. 145) — **INCLUDED IN V1** (SR-3)

Both are **DM / simulation-policy discretionary decisions**. They are part of the V1 sequence at the positions §0 gives them.

> **Discretionary means discretionary.** Neither is a generic user-facing optional-rule toggle, and **no toggle architecture is specified or implied**. The switch in particular is a permission the DM (or, in an unattended simulation, the governing policy) may grant for a given character — not a global on/off setting.

**6.1 Discard (SR-4).** After generation, the player **may be offered** the opportunity to discard the character and roll another when:

```text
no ability score is above 9
        OR
at least two ability scores are below 6
```

**The player may keep the character anyway** — RC states this explicitly (*"a player might want to play this character; if he does, let him"*), and it is part of the rule, not a courtesy.

RC's other, narrower formulation — *"all low scores (all scores under 9)"* — is treated by SR-4 as an **introductory summary**, not an exclusive competing criterion. It is not deleted from the record; see "Rules Cyclopedia Leaves Undefined / Ambiguous" above.

If the character is discarded, generation restarts at §1. If retained, creation continues at §6.2.

**6.2 Score switch (SR-3).** If a player wants a certain class but rolls abilities that strongly favour another, the DM / simulation policy **may** authorize switching the **highest rolled score** into the **prime requisite** ability appropriate to the desired class. This is a **swap of two scores**, not a free rearrangement, and at most one such switch is authorized.

**It runs before eligibility (§0 step 3), so it CAN establish a class minimum** — bounded by §6.3.

**6.2.1 Parameterization of the authorized switch (recorded 2026-09-05).** This records **how the existing discretion is passed to a future pure operation**. It is procedure parameterization, **not a new D&D mechanic, not a new rule, and not a Simulator Ruling** — nothing here changes what RC permits or what SR-3 decided.

Three classes carry **two** prime requisites (Elf: Str/Int; Halfling: Str/Dex; Mystic: Str/Dex), so *"the prime requisite appropriate to the class"* does not name a unique destination. The authorized **source** and **destination** must therefore both be supplied by the caller:

```text
apply_highest_score_switch(
    scores,
    target_class,
    source_ability,
    target_prime_requisite
)
```

The operation validates, at its own level:

```text
source_ability holds a score equal to the maximum score in the ability array
        AND
target_prime_requisite is a prime requisite of target_class
```

**Tied maximum scores need no tie-break rule, and none is created.** Where two or more abilities tie for the maximum, **any tied maximum may be the authorized source**; the DM / simulation policy selects which one, exactly as it already selects whether to authorize the switch at all. **No ability-order tie-breaking, no random tie-breaking, and no automatic source selection among ties** is specified or implied — introducing one would convert a DM discretion RC grants into a mechanic RC does not state.

**Degenerate case — `source_ability == target_prime_requisite`: no valid switch operation.** This is **read off §6.2's existing approved wording**, not added to it: the switch *"is a **swap of two scores**"* (restated by **W6**), and a request naming one ability twice is not a swap of two scores. The operation is therefore invalid rather than a beneficial no-op. **No new rule is created**, and the highest score already sitting in the desired prime requisite simply needs no switch — the character is evaluated on its as-rolled scores (§0 step 4, `W2`).

**None of §6.2.1 alters SR-3, §6.3's envelope, or the §0 ordering.**

**6.3 Envelope of the switch — necessary mechanical consequence.** Because the switch's destination is *a prime requisite*, and because the class ability minimums are fixed (`CHAR-002`), the set of minimums the switch can satisfy is fully determined — **the ruling does not widen it**:

| Class | Minimum(s) | Is the gated ability a prime requisite? | Could the switch satisfy it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dwarf | Constitution 9 | No (prime req: Strength) | **No** |
| Elf | Intelligence 9 | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| Halfling | Dexterity 9 | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| Halfling | Constitution 9 | No | **No** |
| Mystic | Dexterity 13 | **Yes** | Yes, if the highest score is ≥ 13 |
| Mystic | Wisdom 13 | No (prime reqs: Str, Dex) | **No** |

**SR-3 permits the switch to be granted for that purpose** (closing U4 / `CHAR-002` V1). The table remains the hard boundary: **no ruling can make a Dwarf eligible on Constitution 8, a Halfling on Constitution 8, or a Mystic on Wisdom 12**, because the switch's destination must be a prime requisite and Constitution and Wisdom are not prime requisites of those classes.

**6.4 Ordering.** Fixed by SR-3 — see §0. Switch before eligibility; trade after class choice.
